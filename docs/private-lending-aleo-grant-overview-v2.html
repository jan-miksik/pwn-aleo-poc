The applicant is the PWN DAO Foundation, which operates a peer-to-peer lending protocol live on 13 EVM mainnets. Lenders and borrowers agree on fixed loan terms directly, without price oracles or price-based liquidations. This bilateral model fits a privacy-first ZK chain particularly well, because individual loans stay isolated instead of depending on global liquidity, utilisation and liquidation state.

This grant funds a native Aleo implementation of that protocol: funded Elastic Proposals, private bilateral loans, a modular Hub and Config layer, a TypeScript SDK and a reference application — delivered to Aleo mainnet as a usable protocol, not a prototype.

Protocol model

Each loan fixes the credit asset and amount, the collateral asset and amount, the repayment amount and the maturity. If the borrower repays before maturity, collateral is returned. Otherwise the lender can claim it after default. There is no price feed, no health factor and no liquidation: nobody ever needs to inspect anyone else's position, which is precisely what makes the model expressible on a ZK chain where pool-based lending is not.

The Aleo implementation is a native Leo adaptation of PWN, not a Solidity port.

Two outcomes, decided only by the borrower's action and the clock. No third party, no price feed, no liquidation.

Elastic Proposal — the default offer type

In PWN's EVM implementation, an elastic proposal lets one lender publish a single reusable offer with an available credit limit, a minimum and maximum draw, and a fixed credit-to-collateral ratio. Multiple borrowers draw from it independently until the limit is exhausted. Each draw creates its own bilateral loan with its own collateral and maturity. It is not a pool: no shared position, no utilisation curve, no mutualised risk.

Why this is the default on Aleo rather than an optional extra

Aleo transactions have a single signer, and a program cannot spend a private record on a counterparty's behalf. Asynchronous acceptance therefore requires one side to commit capital in advance. Elastic proposals assume exactly that, so what is a convenience on EVM becomes the natural primitive here — and it brings three consequences we would otherwise have to engineer separately:

  • every published offer is backed by real capital and cannot silently become invalid
  • offers live in Aleo public state, so discovery needs no PWN backend
  • multiple draws against one commitment make individual loans harder to link to a lender, so the design is also a privacy measure

Direct offers to a specific counterparty use the same mechanism with a restricted acceptor.

Discovery and indexing

Public Elastic Proposals remain canonical in Aleo public state. The SDK queries that state directly; an optional indexer or cache improves filtering and UX but is never a trusted source of truth. Private user loan records require a separate record-discovery strategy at the wallet layer.

Governance can decide what may be created next. It can never alter, seize or redirect a loan that already exists.

Hub. Registers approved modules for new loan originations: pwn_loan.aleo → ACTIVE_LOAN, pwn_elastic_proposal.aleo → PROPOSAL_MODULE. Future installments, crowdfunding and refinancing arrive as new versioned modules.

Config. Minimal protocol-wide settings: protocol fee (zero in V1), fee recipient, multisig authority, launch caps, and an emergency stop for new originations. Loan-specific economic terms are fixed at creation and cannot be changed later through Config.

Upgradeability. Extensibility and bug remediation are separate concerns. New functionality is introduced as new Hub-approved modules; upgradeability, if enabled, is only a temporary safety mechanism for bug remediation, and custody and settlement logic capable of causing loss of funds should become immutable after audit and stabilisation.

If Milestone 1 concludes the architecture is not viable on Aleo today, we publish the findings and the privacy analysis as an open report for the ecosystem, and the remaining milestones are not claimed.

Milestone 1 feasibility questions

  1. Can lender credit be committed once and drawn asynchronously without lender participation?
  2. Can custody safely support private → controlled state → private asset flows?
  3. What information is public, private, inferable or linkable in each transition?
  4. Does the model work within USDCx compliance and token-registry constraints?
  5. Can Proposal, Loan and token programs compose within practical proving and transaction limits?
  6. What are the measured proving and execution times for the main flows?

Out of scope

V1 is deliberately narrow: bilateral loans with terms fixed at origination. No oracles, no price-based liquidation, no pooled lending, no bridging to PWN's EVM deployments, no KYC or credit scoring. Installments, crowdfunded funding and refinancing are planned extensions. Protocol fees are zero. V1 ships to mainnet as a usable protocol; the extensions follow later.

After the grant

The deployed protocol becomes part of the PWN DAO Foundation's maintained infrastructure and is operated alongside the existing EVM deployments, under the same governance. The SDK is published as an open-source package for third-party integrators. This is not a one-off delivery that ends when the final milestone is paid.

Open questions for the Foundation

  • Security scope Can an external audit or a bug bounty be included as a grant milestone or budget item? Are there auditors with practical Leo experience?
